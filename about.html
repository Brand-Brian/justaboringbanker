Meet the banker

Twenty-three years inside. Here's what I learned.

I'm Ryan Garneau — the Boring Banker. I spent 23 years at TD Wealth watching an industry sell excitement, and everyday people pay for it. This site is what I wish someone had handed all of them.

The problem nobody teaches

Schools teach trigonometry, the periodic table, and how a bill becomes law. They don't teach what a TFSA is, how compound interest works, or why a 2% fee quietly eats a third of a retirement. So people graduate into the most consequential decisions of their lives with no training — and an entire industry standing by, happy to charge them for the confusion.

For 23 years at TD Wealth, I sat on the inside of that. I watched smart, hardworking people make expensive decisions not because they were careless, but because nobody had ever shown them how money actually works. The knowledge gap isn't their fault. It's a design flaw — and it puts everyday people at a huge disadvantage.

"The reason I started this website is because schools never taught us how money actually works, and that puts everyday people at a huge disadvantage. After decades in the industry, I want to demystify personal finance so you can make confident decisions. Everyone deserves the tools to build real stability."
— Ryan Garneau

The banker part

Twenty-three years in wealth management at one of Canada's largest banks teaches you two things. First, the mechanics — markets, accounts, products, taxes, and how they actually fit together. Second, and more valuable: how people behave around money. The panic at the bottoms. The overconfidence at the tops. The quiet power of the clients who did nothing but the boring things, decade after decade, and ended up wealthier than the exciting ones.

That second education is the one this site runs on.

Why "boring"?

Because the strategies that build wealth are dull. Spend less than you earn. Pay down expensive debt first. Buy broad, buy regularly, don't touch it. Nobody sells courses on that — there's no drama in it. So the industry wraps money in jargon and adrenaline instead, and people pay for the wrapping. Excitement in finance is usually expensive. Boring is the edge.

What this site is (and isn't)

This is education — the class school skipped, taught with better props: a YouTube series in the making, four machines that let you drive real market history yourself, and a library of plain-language guides. It is not financial advice. I don't know your situation, your taxes, or your tolerance for watching a portfolio drop. For decisions, talk to a licensed professional who does.
