# BRAND.md — Voice, persona, and visual identity

## The persona

Ryan Garneau is the finance teacher you wish you'd had. A real banker who thinks the
industry sells excitement because excitement is profitable — and boring is what
actually builds wealth.

**Bill Nye translation:** genuine enthusiasm for unsexy things. Props, visual
metaphors, experiments (the calculators are "machines"). Energy comes from clarity,
not hype. He laughs *with* the audience about how dull compound interest sounds —
then shows you why it's the most exciting thing you own.

**One-line positioning:** Financial clarity for the rest of us.
**The edge:** Boring builds wealth.

## Voice rules

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "Excitement in finance is usually expensive." | "Unlock your wealth potential" |
| "The basics school skipped." | "Proprietary wealth frameworks" |
| "Here's what actually happens when you panic-sell." | "Navigate volatile markets with confidence" |
| Plain verbs, short sentences, sentence case | Jargon, all-caps urgency, exclamation marks |
| Self-aware humour about being boring | Condescension, finance-bro swagger |

Never promise returns. Never say "guaranteed." Always educational, never advice.

## Alex — the avatar

Alex is the green-gradient mascot. Usage:
- Appears in the header (logo mark) and hero.
- Acts as a guide/reaction character — like Nye's recurring visual gags.
- Never redrawn or restyled without Ryan's sign-off. Placeholder SVG in
  `assets/icons/alex-placeholder.svg` must be swapped for Ryan's real asset
  (`Alex in Green with gradient fill.svg` from the current site).

## Visual identity

The design concept: **a bank statement that came alive.** Ledger rules, statement
typography, and one playful device — highlighter-marker annotations, like a teacher
marking up your finances.

- Ink: `#10201A` · Banker's green: `#1D4D3B` · Mint gradient: `#7BE0A8 → #2FA872`
- Paper: `#FAFAF7` · Highlighter: `#F5D547`
- Display type: Fraunces. Body: IBM Plex Sans.
- Full token system in `docs/DESIGN.md`.

The highlighter annotation (yellow marker underline) is used **once per page,
maximum** — on the word that carries the joke. Overuse kills it.
