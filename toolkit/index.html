The Toolkit

The official stuff that's actually free.

Canada quietly maintains excellent, free, no-strings money tools — most people just never hear about them, because nobody profits from telling you. Here's the shortlist worth bookmarking, with what each one is actually for.

Official calculators & planners

  • 01

    FCAC Budget Planner

    The federal consumer agency's budgeting tool — build a budget in three steps, save it online, and see how you compare to similar Canadian households. Pairs with our Boring Budget guide.

  • 02

    Canada.ca financial tools hub

    The full government toolbox in one place: mortgage payment calculator, credit-card payoff calculator, retirement income estimator, and comparison tools for bank accounts and credit cards. No ads, no upsells, nothing for sale.

  • 03

    GetSmarterAboutMoney calculators

    From the Ontario Securities Commission: compound interest, debt repayment, emergency savings, and more. Investor education with no product to sell you.

Your government accounts

  • 04

    CRA My Account

    Your real TFSA, RRSP, and FHSA contribution room lives here — not in rules of thumb, not in your bank's guess. Also: notices of assessment, benefit payments, and direct deposit. If you only bookmark one thing on this page, make it this.

  • 05

    My Service Canada Account

    Your CPP contribution record and estimated retirement benefit, EI details, and tax slips. Worth an annual look so retirement math runs on your actual numbers.

Check before you trust

  • 06

    CIRO AdvisorReport

    Look up whether an investment advisor actually works for a regulated firm — and whether they've been disciplined. Takes two minutes and it's the single best fraud filter that exists. Anyone legitimate expects you to check.

  • 07

    CIRO: how to make a complaint

    The formal process when something's gone wrong with a regulated advisor or firm — what to document, who to write to, and the deadlines that matter.

  • 08

    OBSI — the banking & investment ombudsman

    Canada's free, independent dispute resolver when your bank or investment firm won't fix a problem. If the firm hasn't resolved your complaint within its deadline, OBSI is your next stop — at no cost.

Your credit file

  • 09

    Get your free credit reports

    Checking your own report is free and doesn't touch your score. Canada has two bureaus — Equifax and TransUnion — that keep separate files, so check both once a year for accounts you don't recognize and errors that quietly cost you. This FCAC page walks through every free method.

Safety nets

  • 10

    CDIC — deposit insurance

    If a member bank fails, eligible deposits are protected up to $100,000 per category, automatically and for free. Their site has a coverage calculator and a member-institution lookup — useful before parking a big emergency fund at an online bank.

  • 11

    Deposit insurance, explained (incl. credit unions)

    Credit unions are covered provincially, not by CDIC, and the plans differ by province. This overview shows which insurer covers what, so you know exactly how your money is protected wherever it sits.

Everything above is official (government, regulator, or ombudsman), free, and sells nothing. That's the whole admission standard for this page.
